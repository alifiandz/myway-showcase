# Building MyWay

## The product question

How can a student move from “I want to study there” to a preparation plan that reflects their own university choices and preferences?

MyWay’s focus is the personal pathway. Discovery and comparison help a student choose a direction; planning turns that direction into preparation work.

## Work that is easy to miss in a screenshot

**Data modelling.** Institutions, degree programmes, admission facts, verification status, and private operator notes serve different purposes. Treating them as one undifferentiated directory makes later review and access control harder.

**Personalization.** Preferences need explanations and sensible treatment of incomplete evidence. A missing requirement should be visible rather than hidden inside a confident-looking score.

**Continuity.** Planning should survive refreshes and move across devices without silently losing one browser’s changes. Conflicts need a defined resolution path.

**Operator tools.** Keeping university information useful requires a way to inspect sources, edit individual institutions, maintain logos, and identify information that needs review.

**Reliability.** Input validation, account separation, failed requests, deadline ambiguity, and regression tests are part of the product, even when they are not prominent in the interface.

## Documented iterations

The source and migration history reviewed for this case study records work on account profiles and catalogue access in September 2026, followed by planning sync, private review notes, and atomic review operations in October. The October interface work also introduced a visual admin overview, consistent section navigation, individual university editors, and a dedicated logo-editing section.

These are traceable examples of the project’s development. They are not a claim of total hours spent, an uninterrupted commit streak, or a complete historical timeline.
